I've wanted to move to Linux for years. Two things kept stalling me. First,
the sheer number of distros — I'd tried Arch once and found it a bit much,
ran Ubuntu through university (fine, nothing special), and every time I
looked for something developer-focused the options were paralysing. Second,
gaming — or rather Rocket League, once a day, the extent of my hobby.

Omarchy solved the first problem. It's curated by a developer I respect, and
that alone made up my mind. The second problem solved itself: Rocket League
runs cleanly on Linux via the
[Heroic Game Launcher](https://heroicgameslauncher.com). Bonus, not deciding factor.

## Why Omarchy

I'd tried vanilla Arch before — notoriously fiddly. I burned a weekend trying
to install it alongside Windows on a single drive, wrestling with
repartitioning and an undersized EFI partition. Learned a lot, never finished.

Omarchy is an opinionated Arch install put together by
[DHH](https://dhh.dk) (creator of Ruby on Rails). Flash a USB, boot,
done. A programmer-friendly distro curated by a programmer I respect was an
easy sell.

## What I like

- **Tiling window manager** — going back to floating feels broken.
- **Workspaces** — barely touched them on Windows; feel native here.
- **Sensible defaults** — I'm still running mostly stock and it's fine.

It comes with some bloat — DHH bundles his own preferences — but the floor is
high enough that I haven't felt the need to strip it down.

## What's next

Eventually I want to do a vanilla Arch install and tailor everything from
scratch. This is the feet-wet phase. Until then, I'm enjoying having
[Neovim](https://neovim.io) as my daily driver outside of work, with the
[VS Code plugin](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=asvetliakov.vscode-neovim) filling in at the office.
